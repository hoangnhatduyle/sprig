User Guide

Getting comfortable with Sprig

Sprig is a garden planner that runs its own little simulation — seeds you plant actually grow over (simulated) days and weeks, weather affects them, pests show up, and you can step in with real tools like shade cloth, fertilizer, or ladybugs. This guide walks through everything you can do, in the order you'll probably want to do it.

Before you start

Sprig is organized around one big idea: a garden made of beds, and each bed is a grid of cells you can plant into — like little square-foot gardening plots. Time in Sprig moves on its own simulated clock, so plants keep growing, weather keeps happening, and pests keep breeding even while you're not looking at the screen — you're checking in on a living garden, not just editing a spreadsheet.

Everything happens across three tabs at the top of the page: Today, Household inventory, and Manual simulation. The next section covers what each one is for, and the rest of the guide goes through them one at a time.

The three views

Today Household inventory Manual simulation

These three tabs sit at the top of every page. You'll spend most of your time in Today.

  • Today — your main dashboard. See the current simulated date and weather, look at both garden beds (in a flat grid and in 3D), click any cell to inspect or plant it, and control how fast time moves.
  • Household inventory — your seed box and harvest log. Add plant varieties, track how many seeds you have left, and see everything you've harvested so far.
  • Manual simulation — the "hands-on gardener" tools. Install real equipment like shade cloth, preview what a change would do before committing to it, treat pests, and release beneficial insects.

The Today view

Your garden, right now

This is the tab you'll open first every time. It's split into a clock and weather strip up top, your two garden beds (plus a 3D view) in the middle, and a summary or a detail panel underneath — depending on whether you've clicked on anything.

Simulation speed & clock

Sprig doesn't just sit still — it runs on a simulated clock that you control. At the top of the Today tab you'll find:

Fri, Jul 31, 2026 · 9:32:54 PM

▶ Resume Paused ▾ Advance now ↺ Jump to now

The live readout at the top always shows the simulated date and time — not necessarily today's real date.

  • The clock readout at the top shows the simulated date and time. This is what your garden thinks "now" is — it can run faster than real life, so don't be surprised if it's ahead of the actual calendar.
  • Pause / Resume stops or restarts time. While paused, nothing grows, no weather happens, and pests stay put.
  • The speed dropdown lets you pick how fast simulated time passes: Paused, Real time (1 second per second, like a real garden), Fast (about a simulated day every real minute), Very fast (about 12 simulated days a minute), or Max for the fastest catch-up.
  • Advance now asks the garden to catch up immediately instead of waiting for the next automatic check-in — handy right after you change something and want to see the effect sooner.
  • Jump to now snaps the simulated clock back to the real current date and time, without changing whatever speed you had it set to.
Tip

If this is your first time opening Sprig, everything starts paused. Nothing will grow or change until you plant something and pick a speed faster than Paused (or click Advance now).

Just under the clock is a small weather strip showing whether it's day or night, plus sunrise and sunset times. Until the simulation has advanced at least once, it'll say "Weather hasn't been generated yet — advance the simulation to see today's conditions." That's expected — just nudge the clock forward.

Garden beds & planting a cell

Below the clock you'll see one card per garden bed (for example, Left Bed and Right Bed), each showing a grid of square cells — an empty cell is just an open square waiting to be planted.

Click any square to select it — a highlighted border shows which cell you've picked.

  1. Click any empty cell in a bed. A details panel opens beneath the grid for that exact spot (bed name, column, and row).
  2. Click Assign plant in that panel to open the list of plant varieties from your seed box, and pick one. The cell's status becomes Planted right away.
  3. If the cell already has something growing in it, the same button says Change plant instead, and you'll also see an option to add a companion plant alongside the existing one rather than replacing it.

Once a cell is planted, clicking it again shows you everything currently happening there: what stage the plant is at, whether it's stressed, its soil condition, and the care actions covered in Caring for a plant below.

Above the grid, a Show soil moisture heatmap button lets you switch the grid to color cells by how wet or dry their soil is, instead of by plant status — useful for spotting which parts of a bed need water.

The 3D garden

Next to (or below, on a narrower screen) the flat grid is a 3D view of the same two beds, complete with a trellis, a bench, and day/night lighting that follows the simulated clock. It's not just decorative — it's wired to the same data as the flat grid:

  • Clicking a raised bed cell in 3D selects it exactly like clicking the flat grid — the same details panel opens.
  • Plants visibly grow, wilt, or show pest activity in the 3D scene as the simulation advances.
  • It has its own legend button (see below) explaining the colors and icons used in the 3D scene specifically.

Reading the legend

Under the grids, a collapsible Legend panel explains every color and icon you'll see on a cell. Expand it any time you're not sure what a marking means.

  • Planted — seed is in, hasn't sprouted yet
  • Germinated — has sprouted
  • Growing — actively developing
  • Harvested — produce has been picked
  • Removed — plant cleared from this cell
  • Stressed — needs attention soon
  • Critical — needs attention now

The legend also lists equipment icons (shade cloth, grow light, rain cover), pest icons, and predator icons — but only the ones actually relevant to your garden right now, so it won't show a pest icon if nothing has pests yet.

Garden overview panel

When no cell is selected, the space under the grid shows a Garden overview instead of a cell's details — a quick report on the whole garden:

  • Overall stats: percent of cells planted, number of beds, and number of distinct plant varieties in use.
  • Per-bed cards showing how full each bed is, a "Needs attention" note if anything is stressed, critical, or infected, any equipment installed on that bed, and whether beneficial predators are active there.
  • A simple count of how many cells are in each lifecycle stage, and which plants (and how many of each) are growing in that bed.

Click × on a selected cell's panel (or just click empty space) to deselect it and return to this overview.

Household inventory

Your seed box and harvest log

This tab is where plant varieties live before they ever touch soil, and where everything you've picked ends up afterward. It has two sub-tabs: Seeds and Yield.

Adding seeds

  1. Click Add plant in the top-right of the panel.
  2. Fill in a plant name (required), optionally its species, how many seeds you have (quantity), what unit that's measured in (seed, packet, start — whatever makes sense to you), and any notes.
  3. Click Add plant to save it. It now appears as a small square "seed slot" in the grid, showing a leaf icon (or a photo, if you add one) with the remaining quantity in the corner.

Click any seed slot to open a small popup with three actions: Edit its details, Image to upload a photo for it, or Delete it (plants you've already grown from it stay recorded in history even after you delete the seed entry).

The search box above the grid filters by name, species, or notes — handy once your seed box grows past a handful of varieties.

Planting by drag-and-drop

Instead of using the Assign plant button from the Today tab, you can drag a seed slot straight onto an open cell in either garden bed:

  1. From the Seeds grid, press and drag a plant's square onto any cell shown in a garden bed (you'll need both the inventory and the garden visible — this works well on a wider window).
  2. Drop it on the cell you want. A confirmation dialog pops up asking how much of your seed supply to consume, and — if that cell already has something growing — whether to replace the current plant or add it as a companion instead.
  3. Click Confirm planting to finish, or Cancel to back out without changing anything.
Note

A seed slot with zero quantity left can't be dragged — you'll need to edit it and add more, or it's simply out of stock.

Yield & harvest log

Switch to the Yield sub-tab to see everything you've harvested:

  • A running total by plant and unit (for example, "Tomato — 4.5 lb across 3 harvests").
  • A full chronological list of individual harvests, each with the date, which bed/column/row it came from, and any notes you added when you recorded it.

Harvests get added from the Caring for a plant panel, not from here — this tab is a read-only ledger of what's already come in.

On the Today tab, inside a selected cell

Caring for a planted cell

Once you click a planted cell, its details panel shows two areas worth knowing about: Growth and Care.

Growth — what the plant is doing on its own

Germination and growth happen automatically as simulated time passes — you don't click anything to make a plant grow. This panel just reports what's going on: its current stage (germinating, vegetative growth, flowering, fruiting, mature, dying back), roughly how much canopy and fruit it has, and a sustained-stress meter. If something's wrong, a small colored badge names the dominant problem — for example not enough light or drought stress — see the glossary for the full list.

While a plant is in the Growing stage, you can also:

  • Record a harvest — enter an amount and unit (and optional notes), then submit. The plant keeps growing afterward; this just logs what you picked without ending the planting.
  • Finish planting — marks this planting as done for good, once you're finished harvesting from it.

Care — things you actively apply

Further down the same panel, a Care section shows the cell's soil condition (moisture, temperature, nutrients, any mulch already down) plus buttons for hands-on treatments:

ActionWhat it does
Apply mulchLay down a mulch layer at the depth (in mm) you choose — helps retain moisture.
Add compostAdds organic matter that releases nutrients slowly over time, rather than all at once.
Apply fertilizerChoose synthetic (fast-acting, but leaches out of the soil easily) or organic (slow-release), and set how much nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium (N/P/K) to add.
Apply fungicideKnocks back an active infection on that cell — a real treatment, not an instant cure.
Apply weedingReduces weed pressure on the cell — again, a real reduction rather than resetting it to zero.

Every one of these is a real change: unlike the what-if preview described next, care actions actually affect this cell's future growth the moment you apply them.

Manual simulation

Stepping in as the gardener

This tab is where you go beyond individual cells and act on whole beds at once — installing real equipment, trying out a scenario before committing to it, or dealing with pests. It has two sections: Conditions and Pests & predators.

Conditions: equipment and what-if previews

First, tick the checkbox for whichever bed (or beds) you want to affect — Left Bed, Right Bed, or both. Everything below applies to whatever's ticked.

The one thing to keep straight

Install real equipment permanently changes your actual garden's future growth. Preview a what-if scenario never saves anything — it just shows you a projection so you can decide whether it's worth doing for real.

Install real equipment

  1. Pick an Equipment type: Shade cloth (less light), Grow light (more light), or Rain cover (less rain).
  2. Drag the Intensity slider to how strong an effect you want (each type has its own sensible maximum, so you can't overdo it).
  3. Click Install. It now shows up under "Active equipment" for that bed, and you can Remove it later the same way.

This actually changes the selected bed's future growth, starting the next time the simulation checks in on it — it's real gear on a real bed, not a simulation trick.

Preview a what-if scenario

This projects your bed's current plants forward under different conditions, purely to help you decide — nothing here is ever saved or applied to your real garden.

  1. Optionally click one of the three preset buttons — Shade cloth preset, Grow light preset, or Rain cover preset — to fill in realistic light/rain numbers for that scenario automatically.
  2. Or set the numbers yourself: Days to project (how far into the future to simulate), Light and Rain multipliers (where 1 is normal — above 1 is more, below 1 is less).
  3. Click Run preview. For every planting in the selected bed(s), you'll see a one-line before-and-after: growth stage, canopy percentage, fruiting, and any wilting or sustained stress the projection predicts.
Tip

A natural workflow: click a preset to see roughly what shade cloth (or a grow light, or a rain cover) would do, run the preview, and if you like the projected outcome, scroll back up and actually install that equipment for real.

Pests & predators

Same bed-selection idea as above: tick the bed(s) you want to act on first.

If anything's currently active, you'll see it listed right away — Active pests (with a pressure level like low/moderate/high) and, if you've released any, Active predators and their population.

Apply pesticide

  1. Choose which Pest to target (for example, Aphid, Caterpillar, or Slug).
  2. Optionally tick Broad-spectrum — this also suppresses beneficial predators on the same bed, so use it only when that trade-off is worth it.
  3. Click Apply. It knocks that pest's population down hard — but not to zero — reflecting real pesticide use.

Release predators

  1. Pick a Predator: Ladybug (targets aphids specifically) or General Beneficials (covers aphids, caterpillars, and slugs more broadly).
  2. Set an Amount between 0 and 5.
  3. Click Release. Predator numbers then rise and fall on their own afterward, hunting down their prey over time — you're seeding a population, not directly killing pests.

Health & stress

A plant can be healthy, stressed (worth keeping an eye on), or critical (needs attention now). When it's not healthy, Sprig names the single biggest cause — its "dominant stress dial":

  • Heat stress
  • Cold stress
  • Drought stress
  • Overwatered
  • Not enough light
  • Nutrient-limited
  • Transplant shock
  • Wind lodging risk
  • Pest & disease pressure

Equipment

  • Shade cloth — reduces light.
  • Grow light — adds light.
  • Rain cover — reduces rain reaching the bed.

Pests & predators

  • Aphid, Caterpillar, Slug — the pests you can treat with pesticide.
  • Ladybug — preys specifically on aphids.
  • General Beneficials — a broader mix of helpful insects that preys on aphids, caterpillars, and slugs.

Tips & troubleshooting

  • Nothing seems to be happening. Check the simulation speed — if it says Paused, growth, weather, and pests are all frozen. Pick a speed, or click Advance now.
  • I don't have any plants to choose from. Add at least one plant in Household inventory → Seeds first — the Today tab's plant picker is built from your seed box.
  • A seed won't drag onto a bed. It's probably out of stock — open its popup and check the quantity, or edit it to add more.
  • I tried a what-if preview and I want that for real. Previews never save anything — go to Install real equipment and set it up there instead.
  • A cell won't let me plant a fresh crop. If it's marked Harvested, clear or remove it first — Sprig deliberately won't let you plant straight over a harvested cell.
  • Something looks off in the garden view. Click Legend under either the flat grid or the 3D view to check what a color or icon means before assuming something's wrong.

That's the whole tour. The best way to get comfortable is to add one plant variety, drag it into a cell, and nudge the simulation speed forward — you'll see the whole loop in a few minutes.
